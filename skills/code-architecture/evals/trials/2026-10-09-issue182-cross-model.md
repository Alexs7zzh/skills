## Cross-model reading

- **Design concreteness is the fork.** Claude's design committed to forms (a reconcile that calls the setter, a planner returning a motion case, a floor confirmation keyed by generation), so comparers could refute them and did, most consequentially the setter's entry work. Codex's design wrote invariants in the voice of enforcement ("the observer movement owner selects and executes the geometry-limited operation") and said so: "this names the necessary boundary without guessing an engine function signature". Every comparer passed every encoding. A principle cannot be refuted, so the pipeline's main lever never engaged.
- **Over-production is the mirror failure.** Claude counted any refuted statement as a design defect, revised, and recompared seven groups; each round added text-level rows that became handoff "corrections" and ledger rows.
- **Prose.** Codex's report uses abstract nouns ("semantic reconciliation owner", "all-observer effect gate") with no file or routine; a developer cannot start from it. Claude's handoffs name engine routines, line ranges and spec steps.
- **Conventions.** Codex misread the tracker issue's Completion section as a claim. Claude did not.
- **Blocking.** Claude asked two questions mid-run; both answers matched the recommended option. Codex asked none.
- **Slots.** Codex had three child-agent slots and batched; it disclosed this in stages.md.

## Skill changes made from this run

- design.md: an encoding is refutable (owner, member kind, performs, refuses, provider routines replaced); a restated invariant is a defect; commit to a form on a premise rather than leave integration unresolved.
- compare.md: design defect (form or level wrong, or no refutable form) split from correction (bound, cost, lag, wording); "passes" names what it was tested against; only defects trigger the revision; divergence rows carry a Consequence; synthesis writes handoffs only for consequential rows, a Lower rows table for the rest, Defaults taken instead of decisions where a recommendation exists; handoffs use the model's symbols; ledger rows per handoff and form-changing defect.
- SKILL.md: no stage blocks on the user; proceed on the recommended option as a premise and list it.
- extract.md: an issue's completion section is the obligation; shipped work is in dated comments and revisions.
- GOAL.md: values "An encoding commits" and "Consequence decides the output"; maintenance tests under each model.

## Concretization after the trials

The developer asked that procedures depend less on a model's reading of them. Changes, all in the working tree after the trials above:

- **Fixed slots, checked by a script.** `scripts/check_artifacts.py` checks the model (orientation subsections, numbered section headings, row ids, `[S]`/`[P]`/`[M]`/`[U]` tags, Enforcement tokens, a `PR` table, `K` rows citing existing ids and lens slugs), the design (`I` table, `### E<n>` encodings with Invariant, Owner, Member, Performs, Refuses, Replaces, Level, Premises slots, `P` table, every `K` row answered), each compare file (`## Design check` table with verdict and tested-against, `## Divergences` entries with the six slots and a consequence class), the synthesis (section order, six handoff slots, Summary length, Lower rows table, Ledger columns) and each verify file. Run against the day's artifacts: the Claude model fails on the `PR` table, `K` ids, section 9 and 10 column names and id-less rows; the Codex model likewise; both designs fail on the encoding slots; trial A's synthesis passes unchanged; the Claude whole-model compare fails only on tested-against cells and one Consequence slot. So the slots are close to what both models already write, and the failures are the new requirements.
- **Object sweeps in place of judgment.** Extraction gets a `PR` row per provider routine the subsystem calls, overrides or forks, with the roots it writes, and a writer list per state root that cites those rows. Both runs' models missed the mode-entry routine's floor work; a `PR` sweep is the row that would have carried it. Compare judges each encoding by four named sweeps: routines, writers, level placement, shapes. Each divergence names a consequence class from a fixed set.
- **A verify stage.** One fresh verifier per handoff checks the handoff's sequence, routines and seams at the source, facts only, and the synthesizer records the verdict. The ledger's invented rows justify the one sanctioned re-walk.
- **A stage table in SKILL.md:** agent, reads, may open, writes, passing check, per stage.

## 2026-10-10: terms, group delegation, STE pass, fresh reviews

Changes in the working tree after the end-to-end runs above:

- **Terms.** SKILL.md gained a Terms list, one meaning per term across the files: model, row, PR row, K row, section group, lens, invariant, encoding, premise, provider, gate, design check with its three results, divergence with its five dispositions and five consequence classes, handoff, lower row, decision, default taken, verdict, checker. "Couple row" is gone; the concurrent-handoff prefix moved from `H` to `Q` so `H` means a synthesis handoff only; the design-check column is "result" so "verdict" is the verifier's alone.
- **Group delegation at extraction and design.** The developer's concern: one agent extracting and one designing is too little thinking. Extraction now briefs one delegate per section group, each reading the whole bounded source for its sections; the extractor merges and writes the K rows. Design runs in two passes: one designer per section group writes invariants, encodings and premises for its sections; one integrator merges, resolves conflicts (a new Conflicts resolved section), writes Structure and runs the shapes check. The checker has `--group` for group files.
- **Slots fit the input.** Every section and slot has a `none, because <reason>` answer, and the checker accepts them: a model with no PR rows or K rows, a group with nothing to encode, a synthesis with no handoffs. A handoff is at most 600 words; a lower row is one line.
- **Removes slot.** Each encoding states what it takes away from the current path and what then bounds the quantity that behavior governed. This is the slot for the stalled-receipt class of consequence, which no model reasoned to unprompted.
- **STE-derived style.** From ASD-STE100: one term one meaning, one instruction per sentence, about 20 words a step and 25 a fact, noun strings of at most three words, keep the article and the verb, one topic per paragraph. Applied to SKILL.md and design.md as rewrites and to the paragraphs added on 2026-10-09 in extract.md and compare.md; older prose in lenses.md and logging.md is untouched. The same rules went into skill-authoring's Style section.
- **GOAL.md** keeps the why and no longer restates the decision and handoff rules.
- **Fresh reviews.** Two Opus agents with no knowledge of this work reviewed code-architecture and skill-authoring against the skill-authoring checklist (`/tmp/ca-stage3-20261010/review-*.md`). Fixed from them: the checker rejected honest empty results and citations of `O` and `D` rows; the verify rule still demoted a handoff over one wrong line number, so a fact present at another line is now a correction, and a handoff refuted at verify keeps its ledger row; conditional finding and evidence gap now divide by whether the missing fact is about the environment or about the code; consequence classes have an order and a qualification rule, with the no-goals case named; the provider-routine sweep is bounded to routines the subsystem calls directly; `design-revised.md` is the whole design with Revised, Unchanged or New on each encoding. In skill-authoring: a required Review result for review runs, `none, because` in the maintenance result, disposition tokens matching the step verbs, the premise rule's exception written in its sentence, the anecdotes rewritten as present-tense mechanisms with a non-code example, GOAL's one-home value stated as a reason. Left open from the reviews: about twenty long sentences in the older files, the duplicated fresh-context and one-incident bullets in skill-authoring, and the lens examples' commerce and audio vocabulary.
