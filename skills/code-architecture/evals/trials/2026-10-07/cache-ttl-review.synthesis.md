### 1. Room-list removal reaches no store

1. **Outcome and cause.** Room R is in both caches. `rooms.closed` for R arrives, and the next drain runs the removal closure. `Rooms.Invalidate(R)` goes through `RoomDirectoryCache` to `MeteredListingStore`, which declares no `Invalidate`, so the call resolves to the interface's empty default (C# default interface member). Favorites removes R. The room list keeps serving R until a page without R applies (about 60 s plus fetch and drain) or R expires, so G2 fails on every close and the two caches disagree meanwhile. The cause is a missing encoding: a required capability is written as an optional default, and the only record of the forwarding obligation is a class comment that is false. Whole-model facets: if the loop has died (handoff 4), R stays until TTL expiry, and that expiry is itself late (handoff 2). Lookups of R count as hits, so telemetry cannot show the G2 failure (handoff 6).
2. **Remedy.** Target structure, from design I1: each cache owns its store, and instrumentation is an observer the cache notifies after it acts. The observer interface has no default members, so no layer between remover and store can drop a call. Smallest migration, in two steps that each keep the bug class removed. Step 1, which lands alone: delete the default body of `IListingStore.Invalidate` so every implementer must declare it, and add the forwarding `Invalidate` to `MeteredListingStore`. A decorator that omits it no longer compiles. Step 2, with handoff 6: replace `MeteredListingStore` and `LoggingListingStore` with observers notified by `RoomDirectoryCache` after the store acts, which also removes the log-before-forward facet of handoff 5. A4's separate instrumentation survives both steps. Stopgap, if step 1 is deferred: add only the forwarding method and keep the default body. Rejected: a test alone with the default body kept, because the next decorator can still drop the call.
3. **Patch boundary.** `Caching.cs`: `IListingStore`, `MeteredListingStore`, `LoggingListingStore`, `MemoryListingStore`, `RoomDirectoryCache.Invalidate`. Producer: removal closure in `DirectorySubscription` (unchanged behavior). Composition: `DirectoryModule` constructor bindings (L15-16). Siblings: replace and lookup paths keep forwarding unchanged. Preserve: favorites Debug logging and room-list metering (G4, A4). Removing from the room list mutates its dictionary in place, which extends handoff 7's collection-modified case to the room list. Land handoff 7 with or before this one.
4. **Verification.** At the real composition, with a fake push channel and the module's dispatcher: apply a page containing R, fire `rooms.closed` for R, call `Drain`, then assert `Rooms.TryGet(R)` is false and R is absent from `Rooms.Live()`. It fails today and passes after step 1. Compile check: a test-only `IListingStore` without `Invalidate` must fail to build. Seams: injected `IPushChannel`, `MainThreadDispatcher.Drain`.
5. **Readiness.** Ready to implement.
6. **Lens.** required-capability (assurance facet from W-D18).

Merged: W-D1, S-10, C-D1, H-D2, W-D18. W-D18's comment-only-invariant facet is here. Its timer facet is in handoff 8.

### 2. Listing validity restarts at the drain

1. **Outcome and cause.** The loop fetches page P at t0, and listing L has a 30 s TTL. P's closure is queued, the app is backgrounded, and frames resume at t0+300 s. The resume drain stamps L's expiry at t0+330 s, so L shows about 300 s past its service validity, which G1 forbids ("not a longer one"). If the first fetch after resume fails (A2), the overshoot holds for another cycle (whole-model facet). In the foreground the overshoot is transit plus up to one frame. A page from a second loop (handoff 3) arrives older and is stamped fresh. The cause is a missing encoding: the timed fact crosses the adapter as a duration, and no origin travels with it. A1 accepts that queued work runs late. It does not accept a longer display, and the design satisfies both.
2. **Remedy.** Design I2. The service adapter reads the injected clock as `sentAt` immediately before it sends the request and, at parse, converts each `TtlSeconds` into an absolute deadline `sentAt + TTL`. The domain listing carries the deadline and no TTL. `RoomDirectoryCache.Replace` takes deadlines and reads no clock for stamping, so one deadline serves both caches (this subsumes H-D8's per-cache `now`). A page applied late arrives with past deadlines and is invisible at once. Rejected: anchoring on the service's production time from a body field or `Date` header, which needs contract v1 (not in evidence) and skew handling. Also rejected: anchoring on receipt time, which is later than production and still overshoots. Outstanding fact (W-D2, H-D1): `sentAt` precedes production only if no HTTP intermediary serves a stored response. If one can, the adapter reads the response with headers (not `GetFromJsonAsync`) and subtracts `Age`. This decides one line, not the structure.
3. **Patch boundary.** `Listings.cs`: `HttpDirectoryClient.FetchAsync` (clock read, conversion), `RoomListing` and `DirectoryPage` (DTO stays inside the adapter; the domain listing gets the deadline), page-apply closure. `Caching.cs`: `CacheEntry`, `RoomDirectoryCache.Replace`. `DirectoryModule`: pass the clock to the adapter. Siblings: initial fetch, every refresh, and queued pages on resume all take the same path. Push removal is untouched. Preserve: `TryGet` (`<= now` expired) and `Live()` (`> now` live) comparisons. Overlaps handoff 4's parse step, so land the two together or land 4 first.
4. **Verification.** Real wiring with a fake `TimeProvider` and a fake HTTP handler returning one listing with TTL 30. `RunAsync` fetches at once (no timer needed). Advance the fake clock 300 s before `Drain`, then assert the listing is absent from `TryGet` and `Live()`. It fails today (stamped at drain) and passes under the remedy. Seams: injected `TimeProvider` and `HttpClient`, explicit `Drain`.
5. **Readiness.** Ready to implement. The intermediary fact changes only whether `Age` is subtracted. Clock premise PR-1 (clock not set backward, advances through sleep) is shared by code and design, and choosing the clock is a composition-root decision.
6. **Lens.** time-origin.

Merged: W-D2, S-12, C-D3, H-D1.

### 3. The lifecycle has no owner

1. **Outcome and cause.** The phase (constructed, running, shutdown begun, shutdown complete) exists only as `_refreshCts` state, an unretained `RunAsync` task, `_retired` and the push handle, and no check orders the calls. Facets, each reachable by call order with no caller in evidence:
   - (a) `CompleteShutdown` in the running state without `BeginShutdown` disposes the handle and the uncancelled source. The loop keeps fetching every 60 s on a token nothing can cancel and posts pages, which are retained or applied if draining continues. Push closures queued earlier still pass `_retired` and act (W-D3, S-1, S-3).
   - (b) `BeginShutdown` after `CompleteShutdown` throws `ObjectDisposedException` from `Cancel` before `Retire` runs, so the subscription is never retired (S-3, C-D8).
   - (c) `CompleteShutdown` twice disposes the push handle twice. Provider behavior is unresolved (W-D3, C-D8).
   - (d) A second `RunAsync` starts a second loop on the same token. An older page that completes later replaces a newer one and is stamped fresh (handoff 2), and service load doubles (W-D4, S-2).
   - (e) `CompleteShutdown` disposes the source without confirmed loop exit. In the G5 order the token is already cancelled and the loop exits at its next await (H-D7), so this harms only in order (a) (S-5).
   - (f) The module is not `IDisposable`, and quit without `BeginShutdown` has no defined effect. G5 obliges the composition root to call both steps, and no exit path that skips them is in evidence, so this facet is an evidence gap, not a defect (W-D8 over S-4). The remedy supplies the one-call exit at no extra cost.

   The cause is a missing encoding (no single phase root) and a missing gate (no transition method).
2. **Remedy.** Design I3 and I5, owned by `DirectoryModule`. One `Phase` field (`Running`, `Stopping`, `Stopped`), written on the main thread in one transition method. A static start factory constructs the module, subscribes and starts the single loop (on the thread pool, which also settles the evidence gap on the loop's thread), and retains the loop task as a read-only `Completion`. The module has no start method. `BeginShutdown` returns a ticket, and `Complete` exists only on the ticket, so complete-before-begin and a second start do not compile. A repeated begin returns the same ticket, and a repeated complete is refused at the gate. `Complete` disposes the push handle once and disposes the stop source in a continuation on `Completion`. `Dispose() => BeginShutdown().Complete()` covers quit. Rejected as the end state: a phase field with guards on the existing three public methods. It is a smaller patch but leaves misordered calls compilable, and refusing them at runtime is the fallback only if the composition root cannot take the API change. Not included: refusing pages after `BeginShutdown` (see Not retained).
3. **Patch boundary.** `DirectoryModule.cs` (`RunAsync`, `BeginShutdown`, `CompleteShutdown`, `_refreshCts`, constructor), `Subscription.cs` (`Retire`, `Dispose`), the refresh loop entry in `Listings.cs` L46-66. Consumer outside the snapshot: the composition root and frame loop, whose call sites move to the factory, the ticket and `Dispose`. Read that root before editing, since the model excludes it. Siblings: start, both shutdown steps, quit, repeats. Preserve: push-after-begin refusal through `_retired`, including the drain-side re-check (G5), and G5's two-frame span. `Complete` blocks only on handle disposal, which A5 bounds.
4. **Verification.** Compile checks: calling complete without a ticket, and starting a second loop, do not build. Real-wiring tests with a fake push channel, a fake HTTP handler that blocks, and the fake clock: (1) begin, complete, complete again: the handle is disposed exactly once and nothing throws; (2) complete while a fetch is in flight: the stop source is not disposed before `Completion` ends; (3) `Dispose` alone retires the subscription and ends the loop. Test (2) fails today. Driving the loop's tick needs handoff 8's timer seam.
5. **Readiness.** Ready to implement. The patch boundary includes the excluded composition root.
6. **Lens.** state-gate. Facets: one-root (W-D5, S-1, the root cause) and lifetime (S-4, S-5, W-D8, H-D7).

Merged: W-D3, W-D4, W-D5, W-D8, S-1, S-2, S-3, S-4, S-5, C-D8, H-D7. S-5's unobserved-fault facet is in handoff 4.

### 4. The service adapter passes provider exceptions and unvalidated pages

1. **Outcome and cause.** Two outcomes share one cause: the adapter returns a deserialized DTO or a provider exception, and classification happens downstream.
   - Loop death (W-D12, C-D4): a captive portal returns 200 with HTML. `JsonException` passes the loop's filter (which admits only `HttpRequestException` and non-shutdown cancellation), and the `RunAsync` task faults. No refresh runs for the rest of the session, the room list empties as entries expire, and nothing in the module observes the fault (S-5). A JSON `null` body (`InvalidOperationException`) does the same. A missing BaseAddress does it on the first fetch instead of at composition (C-D6).
   - Main-thread throw mid-apply (W-D11, C-D5): a page with a duplicate room id, or a missing listing list, reaches the drain. `ToDictionary` or `Replace` throws, neither cache changes, and the exception escapes `Drain` into the frame loop, whose handling is unknown. Queued removals wait a frame (whole-model: G2 survives that in the foreground). While the service keeps sending such pages, every cycle throws on the main thread.

   The cause is a missing encoding: no owned outcome type and no validated page type at the trust boundary. Nullable annotations are not enforced at deserialization (P).
2. **Remedy.** Design I7 and I8, owned by the service adapter. `FetchAsync` returns `FetchOutcome`: `Fetched(ValidatedPage)`, `Skipped(Transport | Timeout | BadResponse, cause)`, or `Stopped`. One mapping table sits inside the adapter, and an unmapped exception faults `Completion` (handoff 3). `ValidatedPage` has a private constructor and one parse, which refuses a null body, a missing list and duplicate ids, and builds deadlines (handoff 2). The loop branches on the kind only. The adapter takes an absolute endpoint validated at construction. Duplicates refuse the whole page: they break the service contract, and refusal shows in the skip warning. Rejected: keeping the earliest deadline per id, which adds a repair rule. Decision outstanding: the bad-response row. Skip one cycle and warn (recommended: A2 already makes the next cycle the recovery, and nothing consumes a permanent stop), or stop and surface on `Completion`. The structure is the same for either answer. Rejected: widening the loop's catch filter to `JsonException`, a stopgap that keeps provider types interpreted far from the adapter.
3. **Patch boundary.** `Listings.cs`: `HttpDirectoryClient`, `DirectoryPage` and `RoomListing` (DTO private to the adapter), the loop's catch filter (L60-63), the page-apply closure (takes `ValidatedPage`). `DirectoryModule` constructor (endpoint). Composition root (absolute endpoint, outside the snapshot). Siblings: the initial fetch and every refresh share the path. The shutdown cancellation maps to `Stopped`. Preserve: F1-F3 merged into one skip with a warning carrying the exception (A2), and F4's shutdown classification, including a bound that fires at the instant of shutdown.
4. **Verification.** Real wiring with a fake HTTP handler: (1) an HTML 200, then a valid page: today the task faults after the first response. Under the remedy one skip is logged and the next tick applies the valid page (needs handoff 8's timer seam). (2) A duplicate-id page: today `Drain` throws. Under the remedy `Drain` does not throw and the caches keep the previous page. (3) A client without an absolute endpoint fails at construction.
5. **Readiness.** Decision outstanding: the user confirms that a bad response skips like A2's failures (recommended) or stops. Everything else is ready.
6. **Lens.** failure-class. Facets: trust-entry (W-D11, C-D5, C-D6) and external-contract (C-D4).

Merged: W-D11, W-D12, C-D4, C-D5, C-D6.

### 5. Cache writes have no single writer

1. **Outcome and cause.** Page N omits closed room R. At the drain the closure swaps the room list to N, then `IFavoriteRooms.Contains` throws (or, from C-D7, `LoggingListingStore` throws in `LogDebug` before it forwards). Favorites stays at N-1 and shows R until its old expiry, and the exception escapes `Drain` (W-D10, S-9, C-D7, H-D4). Separately, the refresh loop and `DirectorySubscription` hold both caches with public `Replace` and `Invalidate`. Main-thread writing holds only because each write currently sits inside a posted closure, so an edit that calls `Invalidate` from the channel thread compiles and mutates a plain `Dictionary` under a main-thread reader (W-D9, S-8: no instance, the form permits it). The cause is a missing gate: no single writer through which both caches change together.
2. **Remedy.** Design I6. An applier on the main thread holds the only write capability: cache write members become internal, or a write handle goes only to the applier at composition. The loop and the subscription post data messages (`PageArrived`, `RoomClosed`) and hold no cache reference. `Apply` builds both new contents first, with the membership calls inside a catch that logs and leaves both caches unchanged, then commits both. The favorites logging decorator forwards before it logs, or becomes the post-commit observer of handoff 1 step 2. Stopgap, if the applier is deferred: inside the existing closure, materialize the favorites subset before either `Replace`. That fixes the membership split and leaves write access public. Rejected: try/catch around the closure with rollback, which still leaves two writers able to compile.
3. **Patch boundary.** Refresh loop (`Listings.cs` L53-58: post a message, drop the cache references), `DirectorySubscription` (post `RoomClosed`, drop the cache references), new applier called from `Drain`, `RoomDirectoryCache` write visibility, `LoggingListingStore`. Siblings: initial and refresh applies, push removal, teardown. Preserve: FIFO order, and the `_retired` re-check on the main thread for `RoomClosed`. The applier keeps that gate (G5), or handoff 3's phase gives it.
4. **Verification.** Real wiring with a fake `IFavoriteRooms` whose `Contains` throws: apply page N-1, then page N, and drain. Today the room list holds N and favorites holds N-1. Under the remedy both hold N-1. Compile check: the refresh loop and subscription constructors take no cache.
5. **Readiness.** Ready to implement.
6. **Lens.** authority.

Merged: W-D9, W-D10, S-8, S-9, C-D7, H-D4.

### 6. The lookup meter counts what the reader was not served

1. **Outcome and cause.** Entry R expires at T, and no page has applied since. `Rooms.TryGet(R)` at T+5 s: `MeteredListingStore` records `Hit()` on key presence, then `RoomDirectoryCache` sees `ExpiresAt <= now` and returns false. Hits exceed served lookups by every expired-but-present lookup, most while refresh is failing or dead (handoff 4) or after backgrounding. The cause is a missing encoding: the meter sits below the decision it reports, so it never sees expiry.
2. **Remedy.** Design I9. `RoomDirectoryCache.TryGet` classifies once as `Live`, `Expired` or `Absent` and notifies the room-list observer with that outcome. The telemetry observer maps `Live` to a hit and the other two to a miss. `MeteredListingStore` goes away, which is handoff 1 step 2. Rejected: reordering the decorator above the expiry view, which needs a second expiry rule in the decorator.
3. **Patch boundary.** `Caching.cs`: `RoomDirectoryCache.TryGet`, `MeteredListingStore`, `IDirectoryMetrics` adapter. Composition (L15). Preserve: favorites lookups are neither metered nor logged (G4, A4).
4. **Verification.** Fake clock and fake metrics: apply TTL 10, advance 11 s, call `TryGet`. Today it records Hit=1 and returns false. Under the remedy it records Miss=1 and Hit=0.
5. **Readiness.** Fact outstanding: what telemetry means by a hit (goals.md does not say, PR-9). If a hit means "served a live listing", ready. If it means "key present in the store", the current count is truthful and no change is justified, although that meaning contradicts the cache's own answer. The observer form carries all three outcomes, so either answer is one mapping line.
6. **Lens.** truthful-outcome.

Merged: W-D14, C-D2.

### 7. `Live()` exposes a mutable dictionary and a hidden instant

1. **Outcome and cause.** (a) A main-thread reader enumerates `Favorites.Live()` across a frame, for example from a coroutine. A drain applies a push removal to the same dictionary, and the next `MoveNext` throws collection-modified. Handoff 1 extends this to the room list. (b) A reader that holds the sequence keeps filtering with the `now` read at call time, so entries past validity keep enumerating (G1), and nothing shows that instant. (c) A reader on another thread reads the dictionary unsynchronized during a removal; only a doc comment forbids it. No reader is in evidence. The cause is a missing encoding: a published collection is mutated in place.
2. **Remedy.** Design I11, owned by each cache. A published map is never mutated. A commit swaps the reference with a volatile write, and a removal publishes a new map without the key (an immutable map, or copy-on-write, since removals are rare). The project chooses the idiom. `Live()` returns a view over the captured snapshot that exposes the instant it filtered at and still enumerates as listings, so readers compile unchanged. Rejected: materializing `Live()` into a list per call, which fixes (a) but allocates on every call and still hides the instant.
3. **Patch boundary.** `Caching.cs`: `MemoryListingStore` (`_entries`, `Replace`, `Invalidate`), `RoomDirectoryCache.Live`. Consumers outside the snapshot: `Live()` readers (source-compatible). Preserve: `Live()` filtering semantics (`> now`). Land with or before handoff 1.
4. **Verification.** Apply a page with favorites A and B. Take the first element from `Favorites.Live()`, drain a `rooms.closed` for B, then continue enumerating. Today this throws. Under the remedy it completes over the old snapshot, and the view reports its instant.
5. **Readiness.** Ready to implement. Readers outside the snapshot decide severity, not the remedy. A reader that displays a held view past its instant stays outside the boundary (PR-8).
6. **Lens.** authority.

Merged: W-D15, S-11, H-D5.

### 8. Timing constants without reasons, and timers outside the injected clock

1. **Outcome and cause.** `DirectoryPolicy.RequestTimeout` (8 s) and `RefreshInterval` (60 s) are typed and named, but neither has a written reason. A maintainer changes 60 s to 300 s to save traffic, and while handoff 1 is open, closed rooms then linger on the room list for five minutes (H-D9). compare.md treats an unmeasured constant as an evidence gap only when its reason is written, so the missing reason is the finding (H-D9 over W-D17 and C-D10). The magnitudes themselves stay evidence gaps (G3 sets none, and response times are unmeasured). Separate facet: `CancelAfter` and `PeriodicTimer` use the system timer while expiry uses the injected `TimeProvider` (C-D9, H-D10, W-D18). Timers and expiry are never compared, so production has no wrong outcome, and that facet is not a correctness finding. Its cost is that no fake-clock test at the real wiring can fire the bound or the tick, which handoffs 3 and 4 need. The cause is a local omission inside a sound structure.
2. **Remedy.** Write each reason beside its constant, as in design I12. 8 s is a client choice: G3 requires a bound but sets no magnitude, and nothing is measured. The effective bound is the smaller of this and the injected client's `Timeout`, both mapping to a skip. 60 s has no recorded reason, and once handoff 1 lands it sets freshness only. Build the refresh timer and the request-bound source from the injected `TimeProvider`: a bound source created with the provider and linked to the module token, and `PeriodicTimer` with a provider.
3. **Patch boundary.** `Listings.cs`: `DirectoryPolicy` (L12-13), the bound in `FetchAsync` (L30-31), the timer in the loop (L48). Preserve: fixed cadence, immediate first fetch, tick coalescing, and the linked source disposed on every exit.
4. **Verification.** With a fake clock and a fake handler that never completes, advance 9 s: today no timeout fires, and under the remedy the cycle is skipped. Advance 60 s: a second fetch occurs.
5. **Readiness.** The reasons are ready to write. The timer facet needs the framework version, which the model does not pin: the `TimeProvider` overloads of `PeriodicTimer` and of cancellation-source construction are framework-dependent.
6. **Lens.** constants.

Merged: W-D17, C-D9, C-D10, H-D9, H-D10.
