# Independence, the deep run

A second reader who did not form the conclusion, and how two verdicts reconcile. Read when the user asked for a deep run, a review touches a risk surface, or a master dispatched you as a peer in a two-family run. Choose your role first; the Herdr wiring is the last section. A subagent inside a single session is not a peer and does not need this file.

A contested item escalates to a probe; it is never dropped. Reasoning is shared in cross-examination, where it is the thing under attack, and withheld from the fresh attack and the fix review.

Finding and fixing are one pipeline, gated per row: a converged claim is fixed at once, as a shelved change with its red and green runs, and the fix review reads that shelve. Only the check-in waits for the user. A round runs from dispatch to `converged:`; a cold re-review is a new round with fresh sessions.

## Rigor by label

Bug and Restructure rows get the full protocol: cross-examination, the proposal slots, probes, a landing, and fix review of the landing. Composition rows are cross-examined like Bug rows and carry no proposal slots. Hardening and telemetry-quality rows land and are reviewed the same way when they are fixed, and otherwise are carried; they never open a cross-edit and never gate the signature. Nit rows close in one line.

## The deep run

The work is the route's procedure: review.md or diagnose.md, plus the project's own doc. Deep adds these steps around it.

1. **Fan-out floor.** Every lens the change touches runs as its own pass; every changed hunk sits in exactly one sweep's scope; the sweeps together cover every object under Enumerate in good-code.md; in a diagnosis, every cluster has its own investigator. Sweeps are mechanical and belong to cheap agents, one per area, reading every hunk line by line and returning obligation rows, file:line plus the claim, and anything odd. Lens passes, cluster investigations, verdicts, composition and the adversarial pass belong to strong models; enumeration, sweeps and probe running to cheap agents. Dispatch sweeps and passes together and let their results meet in the ledger; in Claude Code, dispatch them as one workflow, each returning rows. Start the mechanical sweep first as the long pole, and never finalize with passes outstanding; if time runs out, name each unreturned pass and the code it leaves unenumerated as an honest gap.
2. **Close the ledger.** The ledger is a SQLite file driven by `ledger.sh`, which sits beside this file; `ledger.sh --help` lists its commands. Every protocol fact lives in it: the rows and their marks, which seat is the scribe, the joint report path, the partition, the imports, and the countersignature. Every notification is sent by the helper from a state change and names the receiver's next command, and every mutating command prints the unconverged count and your next step, so the protocol is read from the ledger rather than remembered:
   - One row per claim: label, the clusters it dispositions, claim, certainty step, evidence path, status, and the slots its label needs. Every row ends in a state from findings.md.
   - One convergence rule for every row, contested included: a row converges when the peer who did not last edit it marks it with `agree`. On a contested row the mark agrees that the named probe settles it. Writing is standing by your text, so you never mark your own edit, and after a cross-edit the mark that converges the row is the author's.
   - Every content edit goes through `set`, which advances the row's revision, clears both marks, and voids any countersignature; a raw edit that forgets the revision or the marks aborts, and a mark is never withdrawn except by such an edit. Rows are withdrawn, never deleted.
   - `verified` needs step 4 and an evidence path; `contested` needs a probe; a Bug or Restructure cannot converge with an empty trigger, impact, cost, or proposal slot, and its test seam states `exists: <path>`, `new: <what must be built>`, or `none: <the architecture finding>`, so a described test is never mistaken for a runnable one. After two cross-edits that leave a row below step 4, the next edit must contest it with a probe or land step-4 evidence.
   - Coverage is checked against the partition: `status` and the render name every cluster no row dispositions, and `converge` refuses while one is uncovered. A row names every cluster it covers in its cluster field, so a row per cluster is not needed.
   - A converged Bug or Restructure with its slots filled is **fixable** at once, and the helper tells the fixer (`fix:`). The fixer records a landing with `land <id> rev=`: the shelve, the red run (the seam's test failing on the pre-fix tree, unless the seam is `none:`), and the green run (build and owning tests with the fix), both existing log files; a row whose revision moved since the fixer read it is refused. A fixer's `flag` on a row blocks landing until a seat edits the row. A content edit after a landing makes the row fixable again once it converges, and the fixer lands again.
   - The seat that did not write the row runs `review <id> rev= fix_rev=` on its landing (`review:`), as a diff, naming the revision and landing it read; conditions go into the row with `set` first. A row that is converged, ruled, landed, and reviewed at that landing is **ready**. After the signature the master records the user's go with `go <ids>`, which approves ready rows at their landing, rows sharing a shelve together; after the check-in the master runs `close <id> changeset=`, which records the changeset with no revision, no mark, and no voided signature. A Nit closes on its changeset alone.
   - A **needs-ruling** row tells the master (`ruling:`) and is not fixable until `ruling=` is set: `ruled: <the owner's answer>`, or `default: <option>` while the owner is asked. A row the owner rules out of the round leaves as **carried**, with `ruled:` and its exit in `decision`.
   - `sign` is refused, by the schema in one statement, while any row is unconverged, a cluster is uncovered, any Bug or Restructure is not ready, or a seat has no current Composition row. It records a boundary-preserving hash of both required seat-notes files, and `converge` refuses if either changed.
   When a route opens a single-session ledger, export `LEDGER_DIR=<fresh run directory> LEDGER_ME=A`, use `init --single`, and use `report` in place of imports, marks, and signature: the slot and coverage checks then run at `report`. The single seat lands and closes its own rows; there is no review to record. A clean quick review still needs no ledger, per review.md.
3. **Compose.** When the set is finished, every factual row converged and every Bug and Restructure ready, each seat composes independently, before reading the other's composition. The helper withholds the peer's Composition rows until you submit yours, and refuses Composition before then. Deduplicate to root causes. Re-verify the top finding in code before accepting it, and list every dropped or downgraded finding with its reason. Then read the findings as a set rather than a list: for each pair on the same user path, ask what holds when both are true at once. Two individually reasonable decisions compose into the severe finding, since a defense removed here plus its replacement inert there is a regression rather than two inert features, and severity, ordering and fix sequencing all shift under composition. Write the result as Composition rows: the comma-separated live factual row ids composed in the cluster field, what the set means for one user on one path as the claim, and the check-in order or changed severity as the decision; or use `cluster=none` for one row saying nothing composes and nothing changes. Then `handoff`, and the compositions are cross-examined like any row. A new factual edit makes prior compositions stale; once the set is finished again, each seat edits or adds its composition. Compose yourself, with the whole set in view.
4. **Fresh attack.** Hand the composed set to a fresh strong model: findings, composed severities, and clean rows in both directions. Fresh eyes means a fresh context, not a re-read by whoever formed the conclusions. The attack re-verifies every finding, and re-examines clean and assumed rows wherever a blocker could hide.
5. **Land, then fix review**, per the ledger bullets above and the Fix review bullet under Peers. In a single session the fresh strong model that reviews the landing cannot record `review`; its conditions are row edits.
6. **Report** per the route, with a `passes:` line per reviewer: `passes: N sweeps, N lenses, N probes, N fix reviews`, naming every pass that did not run.

## Two families

Two frontier reviewers from different model families run the work independently, cross-examine each other directly, and converge peer-to-peer. One fixer edits the tree. The master speaks for the user. In a two-family run the peers skip step 4, since cross-examination is the fresh attack; step 3 runs as Composition rows once the set is finished; step 5 runs row by row as rows converge, the fixer landing and the countersigner reviewing. Choose your role once, before reading any steps, and follow only its section.

| You are | Role |
|---|---|
| The user-facing session, and the Herdr preflight below passes | **Master** |
| The user-facing session, and the preflight fails | **Local reviewer.** Say the runtime is unavailable, then run the deep run in this session with subagents, owning validation yourself: build once and run the owning suite. Never imitate the peer exchange without the runtime. |
| An agent dispatched by a master as a reviewer | **Peer.** Run the work and follow Peers below. `ledger.sh status` tells you whether you are the scribe or the countersigner. Never wire or manage the run. |
| An agent dispatched by a master as the fixer | **Fixer.** Follow Fixer below. You edit the tree; you write no rows. |

### Master

Never perform the work, merge findings, or relay reviewer traffic. Never pre-study the items; read project rulings only when gating. Read this file and the project's route doc for its gathering steps, and nothing else. Every message you write to an agent follows the `agent-messaging` skill.

- **Gather.** Run the route's gathering steps, the ones that produce the input and its partition without judging any item: for a review, the changeset inventory; for a telemetry triage, the export, grouping, cluster list and the input's hash; for a crash-report sweep, the issue list and the event-history dedup into incidents. Run the route's standard probes at freeze time, the ones any peer will ask for, and put their results in the frozen input; the project's route doc lists them. Freeze the result in the run directory and create the shared ledger there: `ledger.sh init --scribe <A|B> --joint <joint report path> --route <review|diagnose> --names "A=<agent> B=<agent> fixer=<agent> master=<your name>" --clusters "<the partition's ids>"`. Send each named agent one test prompt first; a name that fails is fixed before `init`. Both peers receive the identical partition, and the dispatch names it.
- **Wire and dispatch** per the Herdr section: two peers and one fixer, with a wall-clock budget: reports due forty minutes after dispatch, convergence twenty minutes after that. A peer at budget freezes what it has, names each unreturned pass, and carries open items as contested. Round 1 is cold: no peer contact until each report is done.
- **Rulings, asynchronously.** A `ruling:` notification is a question for the user: put the row's decision package, with the default the row proceeds under, in front of the user at their next read, and keep working. The user's answer goes to the scribe as one line, recorded with `set <id> ruling="ruled: ..."`; never restate it, never craft a proposal around it.
- **Gate, then present.**
  - Gate only on the scribe's `converged:`, which the helper sends only when the ledger is at zero and countersigned. `signed:` alone is not a final report, and peer traffic that reaches you before `converged:` is information and never a reason to gate or present. After a bounce, gate again on the next `converged:`.
  - Check `ledger.sh status` for uncovered clusters, then the joint report for: decision packages a non-expert owner can decide from; contested items carried with probes, each contested fix shape carrying its axis; every section the route's report mandates, including a Trigger on every finding, a `passes:` line per reviewer, and the fix table; for a diagnosis, every cluster dispositioned, every cause with its step, every proposed fix with its slots or marked a direction; and the countersignature. The line bound is the scribe's concern, and `converge` warns on it; never bounce for length.
  - Bounce it back to the pair once if anything is missing. Gate large reports by section grep plus targeted reads, because a full dump truncates.
  - State the verdict as one line: `sections: <present> | uncovered clusters: none | countersignature: <who>`.
  - Present the report in the route's shape, plus the ready list with each row's shelve, the ruling queue, the convergence map of agreed, withdrawn, carried and contested-with-probes items, and both retrospectives. Then stop. Check-in starts only on the user's go, and the user may drop a ready row, rule, or stop the loop.
- **On go: check in.** The go names ready rows: record it with `go <ids>`, which refuses a row whose shelve-mates are not named. Check-ins are the user's unless the go authorizes the fixer. You close each checked-in row with `close <id> changeset=<id>`; nothing is re-signed. A row not ready stays shelved for the next go. Cap at 3 rounds.

### Peers

- Run the work independently first, in your own cold ledger (`ledger.sh init --cold`), with no peer contact until `ledger.sh import` moves your rows into the shared ledger and tells your peer; that import is your report. Put separate, non-empty `passes:`, `retrospective:`, and `vote:` lines in `<seat>-notes.md` in the run directory; `sign` requires all three from both seats and `converge` appends the notes to the joint report. Ending your turn after a notification is for when you are still waiting on the peer: if the peer's rows are already in when yours land, continue straight into cross-examination in the same turn.
- **Cross-examine** from the queue `ledger.sh status` shows, row by row, the moment both imports are in. For every row you did not last edit: `agree`, `reject` with evidence at certainty step 2 or better, `contest` naming the probe that would settle it, `set` a correction, or `dup` it onto the row it restates. Fan the examination out with subagents, one per claim cluster, each returning its verdict with evidence, and delegate any probe whose output is bulk; queue probe runs to the fixer, the one editor of the tree, so they batch with builds already running, and take the write-up with `claim-probe`. A needs-ruling row you write states its default in `ruling=` unless proceeding under any option would be unsafe. Work the queue until it is empty, then end the pass with `ledger.sh handoff`, which tells your peer what awaits them; a pass that ends without `handoff` stalls the round. A row you edited returns to your peer's queue, since the mark that converges it is theirs. Fix-review each landing of a row you did not write as it arrives (`review:`); do not wait for unrelated rows or composition. Yielding without a certainty-step citation is not agreement: contest.
- **Amendments** are row edits: each names the corrected value or state and its certainty step in the row itself, never in a message or a line number. The schema ends the argument after two cross-edits; whatever remains is a contested row with its probe.
- **Fix review**, by the seat that did not write the row, per landing as it arrives. Read the shelve as a diff, in a fresh context that gets the diff, the row's claim and seam, and nothing of the reasoning. Two questions, asked of every hunk: for each fallible call, what happens on failure and which branch its error state selects; for the test, whether the red run reaches the shipped path, with `exists:` a path to open. Open the red and green logs. A defect is a condition written into the row with `set`, one edit carrying every condition; the row returns to fixable once it converges, and the fixer lands again. A clean landing is `review <id>`. Wording, labels, and evidence layout are never conditions; an unresolved point is a condition, not open time.
- **Closing a round.** When the set is finished, each seat writes its blind Composition rows and hands off; the peer cross-examines them. With every row converged, every Bug and Restructure ready, both current compositions in, and both notes files complete, the countersigner runs `ledger.sh sign`, which records the countersignature and the seat-notes hash and tells the scribe and master. A later Bug, Restructure or Composition edit voids the signature and stales the compositions; a Nit or Hardening edit, and a `close`, do neither. The scribe then runs `ledger.sh converge`, which refuses an uncovered cluster or changed notes, warns over 300 lines, replaces the joint report, and tells the master. Neither seat types `signed:` or `converged:`; the helper sends them. If your peer or the fixer is silent for 30 minutes, notify the master.

### Scribe only

- Before `converge`, read `ledger.sh render` against the master's gate list.
- The report is the render plus both required notes files. Each notes file has its `passes:` line; for reviews, the signed notes include Goal closure and Domain scenarios. Author no claim into the report that has no row, and keep the whole near 300 lines by leaving the incident roster, verified-clean entries, and evidence in files referenced by path; `converge` warns past the bound. A correction is one row edit; the countersigner then re-marks, refreshes affected compositions, and re-signs.
- After the master's gate, nothing is filed: the rows leave the ledger through the exits in findings.md as the check-ins land, each recorded with `close`.

### Fixer

- You are the one editor of the tree, temporary probes included, and you write no ledger rows. `ledger.sh status` lists the fixable rows; a `fix:` notification names new ones. A peer's probe request is a temporary change under Permissions in SKILL.md, run in the next build.
- First, build once and run the owning suite, logs in the run directory, and tell the master in one line.
- Per fixable row, from `ledger.sh show <id>` and the code: write the test at the row's seam and run it on the current tree. It fails, and its failure is the bug the claim describes. A test that passes before the fix, a seam that does not exist, or a build that fails is `flag <id> note=<the fact>`; the row is not fixable again until a seat edits it. Then apply the fix in the row's shape, build, run the owning tests, shelve the row's files with the row id in the shelve comment, and `land <id> rev=<the rev you read> shelve=<id> red_run=<log> green_run=<log>`. Independent rows share one build and one test run; rows sharing a file share one shelve, landed on each row.
- Landed changes stay applied in your tree until check-in or the user drops them; tagged probes are removed after each run. A `set` after your landing means conditions: read the row again, change what they require, and land again; the same shelve, confirmed, is a landing.
- The run directory gets the red log and the green log per landing and nothing else. Say nothing to the master between landings.
- Check in only a row the go names and authorizes you for; the master closes it. Never build a target the project's contract excludes without a ruling.

## Herdr runtime

Wiring for a two-family run. Read when you are the master of a deep run or were dispatched into one. The method is above; `herdr <command> --help` answers flags.

**Preflight.** `test "${HERDR_ENV:-}" = 1` and a responding `herdr agent` command. Either failing makes the user-facing session the local reviewer.

**Cast.** Reviewer A is Claude Opus at high effort, ledger seat A, and is the scribe by default; the master may give the scribe role to either peer. Reviewer B is Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` at high effort, ledger seat B. The fixer is Codex `gpt-5.6-sol` at high effort, `LEDGER_ME=fixer`, started with the peers. Each runs the work with its own subagents.

**Layout.** One new tab for the run, named short after the target ("review cs 15944" or "triage 2026-09-02"). One pane per reviewer and one for the fixer, side by side rather than stacked, each named after its agent ("opus-reviewer", "codex-reviewer", "fixer"). Rename your own agent to an addressable name first with `herdr agent rename`, since reviewers message you by that name. Start each with `herdr agent start <name> --kind claude|codex --pane <pane-id>`. Parse ids from JSON responses rather than guessing, and use `--no-focus`.

**Run directory.** The frozen input, the ledger and its cold ledgers, the joint report, the seat notes, and validation logs travel as files in one run directory, created fresh per run and named after the kind and the time, under the project's ignored in-project folder when its review doc names one, otherwise under a temporary directory. If a directory for this run already exists, archive or wipe it, and stop any prior attempt's agents before dispatching new ones. `init` copies the helper and its schema into `<run dir>/bin/`, and every later command runs that pinned copy whichever copy was invoked, so a run never sees a helper change. Past run directories are not inputs: no step reads them, and no agent removes them.

**Dispatch.** Carry only what is unique to this run, and point the agent at this skill by name, which is what makes it loadable in its session:

```
Deep <review|diagnosis> of <target>. You are <name>, a peer in a deep run; read the `coding` skill (`$coding`), then deep.md, and follow the Peers role. Frozen input: <path>. Ledger: `export LEDGER_DIR=<run directory> LEDGER_ME=<A|B>`. Budget: report by <time>, convergence by <time>.
```

```
Fixer for a deep <review|diagnosis> of <target>. You are <name>; read the `coding` skill (`$coding`), then deep.md, and follow the Fixer role. Workspaces: <paths>. Ledger: `export LEDGER_DIR=<run directory> LEDGER_ME=fixer`. Check in nothing.
```

The scribe seat, the agent names, the joint report path, and the partition are already in the ledger from `init`, and `ledger.sh status` tells each agent its role. Append constraints the user stated, verbatim, and invent none. Do not restate protocol steps and name no notification strings; this file and the helper carry them.

**Messaging.** `ledger.sh` sends notifications through `herdr agent prompt` to the names recorded at `init`: `report ready:` on import, `ledger:` on handoff, `fix:` to the fixer when a Bug or Restructure becomes fixable, `review:` to the row's reviewer on a landing, `flag:` to the scribe on a fixer's fact, `ruling:` to the master when a row needs the owner, `signed:` on sign, and `converged:` on converge. Each names the receiver's next command. If delivery fails, the helper prints the message: send it yourself. After a peer notification, end your turn, since replies arrive as prompts; a `ruling:` notification to the master does not end cross-examination.

**Waiting.** Never wait on review work: no `--wait`, no polling, no pane peeking, no progress narration. Dispatch, go idle, resume on notification. The rule binds peers too: never `herdr agent wait` on your peer or pump its stdin; `ledger.sh status` between rows is a local query, not a wait. `--wait` with a tight timeout is fine for short control exchanges. One liveness exception: when a prompt reaches you after `signed:` and `converged:` still has not, ask the scribe for `ledger.sh converge`, one control message rather than polling.

**States and cleanup.** Agent states are `idle`, `done`, `blocked` (surface to the user), and `unknown`. Close only panes you created, and never stop the Herdr server.
