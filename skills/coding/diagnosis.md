# Diagnosis

Read for diagnosis at every level, and before developing a judged proposal. Seek useful improvement even when the incident's cause remains unknown. This procedure selects no agents or new artifacts and grants no authority beyond SKILL.md.

## Investigate

1. Establish the situation: the intended operation and handling, the observed difference, what failed and what still works. Identify the target version, affected consumer and the source of the required behavior. Separate checked facts, hypotheses and missing facts.
2. Answer the Shared assessment below from available source, configuration, retained evidence and applicable documentation. Inspect the owned stages and inputs that could change the decision. Name the files read and their verdicts; keep uninspected areas explicit. With only a log or an unavailable prerequisite, name the bounded checks still needed instead of inventing deployed settings or product policy.
3. Apply every matching response rule. Resolve an unknown that changes the next action with a useful check, or retain the precise question and available input for a bounded investigation. Before new capture or reproduction, inspect available static evidence and whether owned inputs can drive the disputed branch. If examining file-loading, retry or recovery evidence, use [logging.md](./logging.md) for its design questions.
4. Conclude using the completion rule below. Lead with what happened and the recommended work, its benefit and limits. For judged proposals, answer: **Has it shown up before? What can we improve now? What supports that action? What remains unresolved? What would let us choose the next action?** For those proposals, name the recurrence, issue or landed repair checked, or say it was not checked. Combine answers sharing evidence.

Keep the answers in the existing report or note. In normal work, report the recommendation, deciding facts and material limits once. Combine concerns settled by the same facts. When no action-changing uncertainty remains, give a short explanation without reproducing the checklist questions or unused responses. Normal work creates no separate assessment artifact. An obvious cause needs no quota of alternative theories, but it does not settle recovery, feedback or evidence by itself. Judged work places these answers in proposal.md's slots.

## Shared assessment

This section is the common standard for the investigator and the judge. It ends at the end of this file. Before selecting a response, establish which required user outcome or system obligation makes the issue worth pursuing. Treat its current mechanism and proposed remedy as candidates, not requirements to preserve or replace. If their purpose or necessity is unsettled, make that a deciding question before designing their repair or replacement.

For each concern, supply checked evidence or identify the missing fact, what available evidence was checked, and how the answer could change whether work is needed, its scope or its owner. An unresolved question needs a bounded check that distinguishes the alternatives, or the prerequisite preventing that check. Resolve it now when the available evidence and task scope permit; otherwise propose that investigation and leave the dependent implementation undecided. An inapplicable concern closes with the fact that makes it inapplicable, not a bare `N/A`. Answers may share prose and evidence.

### Concerns

1. **Cause and classification.** Identify the reason established at the owning stage and separate it from hypotheses about its origin. For an unresolved library or service error, cite the official definition of the native code for the actual operation and provider chain. Separate transport completion, protocol status and wrapper outcome. Cover documented causes that change handling or the next action; exclude one only with checked evidence, otherwise leave applicability unknown. A documented possibility does not attribute the incident. Classify the reason and its handling separately: an accepted reason does not prove correct handling or make every failure with the same event name safe. Accepting a checked outcome need not explain an external cause that changes no decision.
2. **Design.** Establish whether the owned code meets the required outcome on ordinary and interrupted paths. Name the relevant contract and its source: an owner ruling, documented contract or checked producer/consumer behavior for a supported operation. Source-established system contracts suffice for routine correctness and safety; a separate policy document is not required. Explain the fit and costs of the relevant choices. A different possible design alone is not a defect.
3. **User loss.** Identify what stops working or is lost, for whom, how often and for how long. Distinguish temporary, persistent and irreversible loss. Separate measured impact from possible impact and unknown duration; log rows do not count failures. For missing identity, check build target, startup timing, when identity is attached and possible test producers before inferring player impact or test origin. Keep unknown provenance explicit.
4. **Recovery.** Identify what restores the required user outcome or ends the attempt safely, and whether it was observed, checked only in source, or neither. Cover applicable exhaustion, cancellation, target change, departure and recurrence after recovery. A success callback, unrelated resumed work or ended attempt does not prove usable recovery. For repeated recovery, check reason selection, waits and spreading, limits and timeouts, reset after success, duplicate effects and restoration of usable state. A known cause or planned repair does not establish that existing affected users recover. For a single-session anomaly on a frequently exercised path, ask whether that client resumed and stayed usable, and what would show it, before repeating a test of the normal mechanism. Use project runtime assumptions to prioritize a local stall or crash hypothesis when supported; occurrence frequency alone does not establish that cause.
5. **Feedback.** State what the affected user sees while impaired and at the final outcome, against the accepted product policy. Name an unmet obligation before calling feedback defective; an alternative presentation is a policy choice. Explain the accepted limitation and its cost. Notification itself does not restore the capability.
6. **Evidence.** Identify the available source, retained-target or configuration checks that can decide the next action before adding diagnostics. For failed shared-file work, check effective paths and writers first. Establish whether existing reports identify the operation, stage, reason and target, connect attempts, measure relevant loss and distinguish usable recovery from abandonment or permanent failure. For each unresolved action-changing alternative, name the distinguishing value, its documented meaning, its producer, where it is retained and which action the result selects. Verify a proposed field's delivery path and name what it still cannot distinguish, including generic or cached errors. A correlation ID enables lookup, not attribution. For repeated recovery, identify the episode from first impairment to verified restoration or a terminal ending. Cancellation or shutdown is a non-recovered ending; a missing ending or lost process leaves the result unknown. When duration or ending changes the decision, name the reports covering the first and changed causes, attempt count, elapsed impairment and ending. For a reused recorder, give its opening condition, first report, closing conditions and retained fields; account for failure, recovery, departure or a changed cause before it opens. An application session alone does not establish that coverage. Rate limiting must preserve changed causes, terminal failure and recurrence after recovery. Reuse sparse evidence when it answers the decision; a missing report matters when a decision needs it.

### Responses

Evaluate each row against the assessed concerns and apply every matching rule. The rows select work, not defects to find. Supported compatible responses accumulate; mutually exclusive product choices remain alternatives. An unknown condition needs a deciding check or an explicit limit, not an assumed negative answer.

| Assessment result | Response and required basis |
| --- | --- |
| Checked code violates the required outcome | Repair the demonstrated defect. Name the violated contract, checked mechanism, value of repair and validation of the affected user action. |
| A supported change can reduce failure likelihood, duration or consequence within the requested goal | Mitigate, contain or improve recovery. Name the owned seam, expected benefit, cost and verification. A known or unknown cause neither requires nor prevents this response. |
| The owner requested a design comparison, or a choice affecting outcome, scope or material cost remains unsettled | Compare the choices and tradeoffs now, naming the question the comparison settles. |
| A remaining uncertainty changes the next action | Investigate using retained identifiers and evidence or an owned-input check. Name the deciding question, available input and which result selects which action. An unavailable lookup leaves the fact unknown, not proved absent. |
| Current evidence cannot answer a needed decision about cause, severity or ending | Improve evidence at the owner that knows it. Name the missing fields or transitions, their consumer, limits and decision value. Prefer an existing facility that answers the question. |
| User feedback is missing or misleading against an established obligation, or a supported policy alternative would help | Correct the demonstrated defect, or offer the policy choice with its benefit and user cost. |
| The exact reason and handling are accepted | Apply the project's reporting policy and the forwarded-log triage rule below when applicable. State the exact predicate, reports retained for unknown, unmapped, contract-breaking and still-actionable outcomes, and visibility cost. |
| Existing design, evidence and feedback meet the goal | Retain them with the facts supporting that conclusion. In forwarded-log triage, also settle the report's destination under the rule below. Other assessments need no invented work to fill an action category. |

For each selected response, state its useful outcome, supporting evidence, material cost or risk and verification. Keep its disposition separate: recommend the action now, name the bounded investigation needed to choose it, identify the owner decision or prerequisite blocking it, or justify retention or deferral. Recommend useful work within the requested scope; implementation and publication still follow the user's authority. Filing or updating an issue records the response and does not itself handle the incident.

A mitigation, evidence improvement or intended policy change needs a supported benefit, cost and validation, not an invented contract violation or historical attribution. An incident can remain unattributed while a separately demonstrated defect merits repair. For a changed limit, timeout or retry delay, name what the value controls, the goal it serves, why the proposed value helps and its tradeoff. Honor an owner-selected mitigation or investigation without demanding an unproven alternative cause be excluded first. External ownership alone does not put the supported user experience out of scope.

An evidence improvement can proceed while handling remains unresolved; keep those concerns separate. Closing a handling concern needs checked contract compliance and, where loss remains, the recovery, fallback or feedback and policy accepting that loss. Neither an expected trigger nor absent reported impact supplies that basis. A wording change earns its place by correcting classification, identifying the next action or preventing a misleading explanation.

Outside forwarded-log triage, for a deferred unresolved concern, explain why useful static checks and bounded improvements should stop now, the cost of accepting uncertainty and the observable result that would reopen the decision. Name the signal and where it reaches its consumer, or explicitly accept that the condition will go unseen and explain why. More identical reports can establish reach without supplying a missing causal distinction. If another occurrence would leave the same decision blocked, select the smallest useful check or evidence improvement, or justify accepting that limit. An inapplicable action needs only its factual basis; it needs no invented reopening signal.

A committed check must answer a remaining question or verify a required obligation, with an outcome that can refute the claim. Predicting its result does not make it invalid. A tautological check, or repeating an already-settled fact while leaving the deciding question unanswered, does not advance the investigation. Ordinary regression validation can still verify that the implementation preserves required behavior.

### Forwarded-log triage

Apply this rule when deciding the response to reports forwarded to engineering. First filter exact signatures already covered by an existing pending issue and its temporary triage-ledger entry. Matching text with new source or schema evidence outside that issue's scope still enters new triage. Continue the covered issue's work; matching reports need no new proposal or repeated assessment. For every current report entering new triage, select one or more concrete responses below. These are responses, not claims that every report reveals a product defect. Use the project's names when it defines equivalent kinds.

| Response | Selection and required fields |
| --- | --- |
| **Fix** | A supported repair or mitigation improves the failed outcome. Fields: the checked mechanism, with file and symbol at the changeset read; what the code does and what it should do instead; the benefit; one check that fails before the repair and passes after it. |
| **Diagnostics** | An unresolved useful failure needs a bounded investigation or distinguishing evidence. Fields: the deciding question; a table with one row per field or check giving its possible values and the action each value selects, plus a row for a value none of them anticipates, with no two rows selecting on overlapping values; the producer and consumer of each field. Prefer available source or retained evidence before new collection. |
| **Suppress** | The exact report is verified non-actionable or expected with acceptable handling, kept local through producer severity or filtering. Fields: kept local when, as the exact predicate the producer or filter evaluates; still forwarded when; and what an input the predicate cannot classify evaluates as, which is forwarded. Acceptance of the failure alone does not justify suppressing unknown or defective handling. |

For a reporting response, choose the applicable [concrete proposals in logging.md](./logging.md#concrete-reporting-proposals); that file owns causal context, recovery boundaries and producer acceptance predicates.

Write each selected response's fields as a table in the proposal's Recommendation. Every response also states its completion: a landed change, or a check that fails today, never a count of future reports. A reopen signal names a report that still reaches engineering after the change; a signal the change itself suppresses is not one.

Supported responses can accumulate for separate concerns. Waiting for data belongs inside a Fix or Diagnostics task, whether an existing pending one or one this triage selects, never as its own action or proposal. That task names the data or signal, its delivery to a responsible consumer, the collection window or review condition, and the decision and exit action for each outcome, including missing input or expiry. More identical rows cannot replace a missing distinction. Pending input cannot postpone independently supported work. Indefinite monitoring, no action, and permanent ledger acceptance do not close a current forwarded report. Implementing a reporting response still follows the task's authority; a read-only triage recommends it without editing product code.

Before treating recurrence as unresolved work, compare the report's build and source path with the landed repair. Old-build incidents fixed in current code are rollout lag, not grounds to reopen the defect. A functional fix can retain identical log text; the template alone proves neither recurrence of the bug nor lack of a repair. Keep any needed rollout follow-up with the existing repair task and its bounded completion check.

When a triage ledger tracks issues and temporary ignores, treat it as the open-work set, not an acceptance archive. Issue closure deletes its pending entry and ignores. A new report then receives the build/source check and response selection above.

### Completion

Finish when every applicable concern is accounted for by supported response(s), a bounded deciding check, or justified retention or deferral. Forwarded-log triage also requires the response selection above. Other assessments can conclude with a concise supported no-change summary. Finding one useful response does not close another concern. Cover alternatives that change the decision, not every failure a dependency could ever produce or every future implementation branch. A checked, intentionally handled reason needs no new research or telemetry merely to fill this assessment.
